# sting
B: Part three. Polygenic risk scores.
A: Same GWAS, different question. MR asks, does X cause Y? A risk score asks, who is at higher risk? MR gives one causal estimate for a population. A risk score gives one number per person. For each person, the sum over SNPs of the number of effect alleles times the GWAS beta. For a disease, betas are log odds ratios, so the score is on the log odds scale. And you need two independent datasets. A discovery GWAS for the betas, and a target sample to compute and test the score. If the same people are in both, the score looks better than it is. Which SNPs? Raw GWAS output has two problems. Most betas are imprecise, the top ones inflated by winner's curse. And SNPs in LD repeat the same signal. The classic fix is clumping plus thresholding. One SNP per region, then those below a p-value cut off. The alternative is shrinkage, which keeps more SNPs but pulls each beta towards zero.
A: Predict first. Pleiotropy in a risk score. Villain or friend?
B: Villain. It was the villain in MR.
A: Friend. Any predictive signal helps. And weak SNPs are welcome too. In MR, each instrument must be valid on its own. In a score, only the sum has to predict. The weak learners from statistical learning.

B: How do you evaluate a score?
A: Discrimination, the AUC. The probability that a random case scores higher than a random control. Calibration, do predicted risks match observed? And added value. Age, sex, smoking and blood pressure, with and without the score. Usually small. A coronary score might move the AUC from zero point seven five to zero point seven six. Highly significant, barely useful.
B: Then why is everyone so excited?
A: Barely useful on average isn't useless. At the extremes, risk can be several times higher. It's cheap, it never changes, and you can measure it at birth. But it partly works through LDL and blood pressure, so in a full model it adds less than you'd hope. And in the obesity example, most people with obesity still have an average or low score. Accuracy is capped by SNP based heritability.
