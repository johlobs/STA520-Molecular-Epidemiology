B: Background correction.
A: The method is normexp. Observed intensity is a true signal plus background. The background is normal, the signal exponential. You estimate the expected true signal given what you saw. Unlike just subtracting, you always get positive values, and low intensities become more reliable.
B: And quantile normalization.
A: Three steps. Within each sample, rank the values. For each rank, take the mean across all samples. Replace each value with the mean for its rank.

# sting
B: Why does the ordinary t-test fail?
A: The t-statistic divides the mean difference by a standard error, and the variance in it is estimated separately for every microRNA. With three versus three samples, that's four degrees of freedom. Typical for omics. And three things go wrong at once. The variance estimate is very noisy. Some features get a tiny variance purely by chance. It sits in the denominator, so t explodes even when the difference is small. A false positive. Other features get a randomly large variance, so real effects are missed. Lost power. The solution is the moderated t-statistic, from the R package limma. Instead of trusting each feature's own noisy variance, limma uses empirical Bayes to share information across all features. Each variance is shrunk towards a common variance estimated from the whole dataset. In words, the moderated variance is a weighted average of the common variance, weighted by prior degrees of freedom, and the feature's own variance, weighted by its residual degrees of freedom.
B: Predict first?
A: Predict first. What happens to the shrinkage with many replicates?
B: It gets stronger? More data, more sharing.
A: The other way. With few replicates, the common prior dominates, and each variance is pulled hard towards it. With many replicates, the feature's own data dominate, and the moderated variance converges to the ordinary sample variance. That's marked exam.

Sharing information across the dataset, variance shrinkage, stabilizes the variances. Fewer false positives, more power, more reliable results after correction.

A: Three steps. Reproduce them exactly. One. Sort the p-values from smallest to largest. Two.
